# US Tax Rules for NRA Investors — Dominican Republic Residents
**Investor profile:** Non-Resident Alien (NRA), Dominican Republic tax resident, no US–DR tax treaty.
**Last reviewed:** May 2026 | *This is a reference summary — not legal/tax advice. Consult a qualified US tax advisor for your specific situation.*

---

## Overview

As a Dominican Republic resident investing in a US brokerage account (TradeStation), you are subject to US withholding tax on certain types of US-source income. The key features of your situation:

1. **No tax treaty** between the US and DR → default statutory rates apply.
2. **Capital gains are generally exempt** for NRAs on publicly traded securities.
3. **Withholding is handled automatically** by the broker (TradeStation) — you do not file a US tax return unless you have income not covered by withholding.
4. **W-8BEN** must be on file with TradeStation — renew every 3 years.

---

## Rate schedule by income type

| Income type | US statutory rate | Your rate (no treaty) | Applies to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary dividends | 30% | **30%** | VTI, VEA, VWO distributions |
| REIT distributions | 30% | **30%** | VNQ (and any REIT ETF) |
| Treasury interest | 0% | **0%** | SGOV, SHY, SCHP (see below) |
| Corporate bond interest | 30% | **30%** | VCIT distributions |
| Capital gains (short-term) | 30% | **0%*** | ETF sales |
| Capital gains (long-term) | 20% | **0%*** | ETF sales |
| Commodities/futures gains | varies | **0%*** | PDBC |
| Qualified dividends | 15%/20% | **30%** | NRAs do not get reduced rate |

*NRAs are generally not subject to US capital gains tax on publicly traded securities, provided they are not present in the US for 183+ days in the tax year and the income is not effectively connected to a US trade or business.*

## Key exemptions in detail

### Treasury interest — portfolio interest exemption (IRC §871(h))

US Treasury securities (and ETFs that hold only Treasuries, such as SGOV and SHY) qualify for the **portfolio interest exemption**:
- No US withholding on interest income from registered US obligations.
- Applies to SGOV, SHY, and SCHP (TIPS — both coupon and inflation adjustment components).
- **Condition:** Broker must have a current W-8BEN on file.
- **Result:** 100% of Treasury interest reaches your account. This is a major tax advantage vs. corporate bonds.

### Capital gains exemption (IRC §871(a)(2))

NRAs are not subject to US capital gains tax on the sale of publicly traded securities, provided:
1. You are not physically present in the US for 183+ days in the tax year.
2. The gains are not effectively connected to a US trade or business.
3. The securities are not US real property interests (USRPI).

**REITs and FIRPTA (§897):** Gains from selling REIT ETFs like VNQ *may* be subject to FIRPTA withholding if the REIT is a "US Real Property Holding Corporation." However, publicly traded REIT ETFs held by diversified investors typically do not trigger FIRPTA on the *sale of ETF shares* (as opposed to direct real property sales). Consult your tax advisor if selling a large position.

## ETF-by-ETF tax treatment

### VTI — Vanguard Total Stock Market ETF
- **Distributions:** 30% withheld (ordinary dividends + some return of capital).
- **Sales:** 0% capital gains (NRA exemption).
- **Yield ~1.3%** → relatively low withholding drag vs. high-yield alternatives.
- **Action:** Hold for total return. Don't prioritize dividend reinvestment.

### VEA — Vanguard FTSE Developed Markets ETF
- **Distributions:** 30% US withholding. Additional foreign withholding may apply at source (e.g., German stocks may have German WHT before US WHT).
- **Yield ~3.0%** → meaningful drag. Net after 30% ≈ 2.1%.
- **Foreign tax credit:** Not claimable by NRAs with no US income tax filing obligation.
- **Action:** Limit to IPS target. Don't overweight.

### VWO — Vanguard FTSE Emerging Markets ETF
- **Distributions:** 30% US withholding + potential local-country withholding on underlying dividends.
- **Yield ~2.8%** → net after 30% ≈ 1.96%.
- **Action:** Hold for long-term EM growth exposure. Cap at IPS target.

### SGOV — iShares 0-3 Month Treasury Bond ETF
- **Distributions:** **0% withholding** (portfolio interest exemption).
- **Yield ~5.2%** (tracks Fed Funds — update quarterly).
- **Sales:** 0% capital gains.
- **Action:** Maximum tax efficiency for this investor. Preferred cash management tool.

### SHY — iShares 1-3 Year Treasury Bond ETF
- **Distributions:** **0% withholding** (portfolio interest exemption).
- **Yield ~4.5%** (short end of yield curve).
- **Sales:** 0% capital gains.
- **Action:** Tax-efficient fixed income. Prefer over VCIT when fixed income is underweight.

### VCIT — Vanguard Intermediate-Term Corporate Bond ETF
- **Distributions:** **30% withheld** (corporate bond interest paid to NRA is not exempt).
- **Yield ~4.8%** → net after 30% ≈ 3.36%.
- **Comparison:** SGOV yields ~5.2% with 0% withholding. On an after-withholding basis, SGOV currently outperforms VCIT for this investor.
- **Why hold VCIT:** Credit spread exposure, diversification, and yield-curve positioning justify the allocation. But do not overweight.
- **Action:** Maintain at 10% target. Do not increase when fixed income is underweight — prefer SGOV/SHY first.

### SCHP — Schwab US TIPS ETF
- **Distributions:** **0% withholding** (TIPS are Treasury obligations; portfolio interest exemption applies to both coupon and inflation-adjustment components).
- **Real yield ~2.5%** + inflation adjustment.
- **Inflation-adjustment:** Generally treated as additional interest income, also exempt.
- **Sales:** 0% capital gains.
- **Action:** Excellent inflation hedge + zero withholding drag. Highly recommended for this profile.

### VNQ — Vanguard Real Estate ETF
- **Distributions:** **30% withheld** (REIT distributions are treated as ordinary dividends for withholding purposes).
- **Yield ~4.0%** → net after 30% ≈ 2.8%.
- **Annual withholding drag** on a $70,000 position (7% of $1M portfolio): ~$840/year withheld.
- **FIRPTA on sale:** ETF shares of a publicly traded REIT ETF are generally not subject to FIRPTA withholding on sale for NRAs (subject to change — verify with advisor if selling).
- **Action:** Cap at 7% IPS target. Valuable for diversification and real asset exposure, but tax-inefficient. Treat as a diversification cost, not an income tool.

### PDBC — Invesco Optimum Yield Diversified Commodity Strategy ETF
- **Structure:** C-corporation (NOT a partnership/limited partnership).
- **Tax form:** Issues **1099** (not a K-1) — eliminates partnership tax complexity.
- **Distributions:** Minimal; most returns are capital gains (0% for NRA).
- **Futures income:** Treated as capital gains → 0% for NRA.
- **Withholding:** **0%** on typical PDBC distributions.
- **Annual check:** Confirm PDBC has not changed its legal structure. If it converts to a partnership, a K-1 will be issued and tax treatment changes materially.
- **Action:** Most tax-efficient way to add commodities exposure for an NRA.

## W-8BEN compliance

| Requirement | Details |
|---|---|
| Form | IRS Form W-8BEN |
| Where filed | With TradeStation (not IRS) |
| Validity | 3 years from signature date |
| Purpose | Certifies foreign status, enables 30% statutory rate (vs. 31% backup withholding) |
| Action | Renew before expiration. TradeStation will notify you. |

If W-8BEN expires, backup withholding at **24%** may apply to all proceeds — worse than the 30% dividend rate for long-term holders but applied to gross proceeds including return of principal. Keep this current.

## Dominican Republic tax obligations

This document covers US-side withholding only. You may have DR-side obligations on:
- Foreign-source income received in DR (dividends, interest)
- Unrealized or realized gains if DR implements capital gains taxes
- Currency exchange gains

**Consult a Dominican tax advisor** for your local obligations. The US and DR do not share tax information under an automatic exchange agreement (as of 2026 — verify annually).
